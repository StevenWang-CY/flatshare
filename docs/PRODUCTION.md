# Running a voice agent beside background work on one GPU

This guide is for an operator who serves a realtime voice agent (speech to text, an LLM, text to speech) from their own GPU with vLLM, and wants the same GPU to run background LLM work: coding agents, post-call summaries, batch jobs. It gives the configuration E2 measured, what each part buys, what to watch and how it fails. The numbers come from one RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB serving Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507-FP8 beside Whisper and Kokoro ([BENCHMARKS.md](BENCHMARKS.md#voice-under-agent-load-e2)), and an A100 SXM4 80 GB ran the same comparison on 2026-10-26 ([the A100 run](HARDWARE.md#what-it-measured-2026-10-26)). The mechanisms are not specific to the card, though what each costs is; E1's regime map places other hardware by a model ([METHODS.md](METHODS.md#what-transfers-to-other-hardware)).

## What it buys

Two callers, 25 turns each, beside 24 coding-agent sessions whose contexts fill the GPU's KV pool (E2's discriminating scenario). Voice-to-voice latency (V2V) runs from the end of the caller's speech to the agent's first audio, read from waveforms; the SLO is voice alone plus 250 ms at p95, 2,034 ms. Medians of W5's runs, each round on its own sample of the agents' traces (a configuration more than one campaign ran is pooled over them), with the p95's range:

| Configuration | Runs | V2V p95 | Replies over the SLO | Agents' tokens/s, first 240 s | Agent TTFT p99 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Voice alone | 5 | 1,784 ms (1,764–1,819) | 1 / 295 | — | — |
| Shared, tuned vLLM 0.32 (priority, step cap 512/256, a CPU KV tier) | 10 | 10,477 ms (8,686–12,115) | 532 / 590 | 3,305 | 106 s |
| Shared, abort-and-resubmit in a router, the same engine | 5 | 2,522 ms (2,516–2,868) | 130 / 295 | 3,332 | 193 s |
| **Shared, this configuration at the box's 512/256** | 15 | **2,236 ms (2,132–2,397)** | 286 / 885 | 3,203 | 111 s |

On stock vLLM the shared GPU leaves the voice p95 at over five times its SLO: a waiting voice turn cannot take KV from running agents ([vLLM #40004](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/40004)), and once it runs, agent prefill chunks share every step of its reply. With this configuration the tail comes within about 0.2 s of the SLO (202 ms at the median), the agents keep 97% of their token rate over the calls' first 240 s, and their TTFT p99 stays within the runs' spread. The SLO is still missed: it allows 5% of the replies over its threshold, and 32% of this configuration's are over it, against 90% on tuned vLLM. A deployment that must meet it admits less background work beside the calls: on the serving profile, four of this scenario's sessions beside two calls, the envelope that [Two cards](#two-cards) gives. Capping every reply at 16 tokens in the gateway as well (arm 9, make-room with the lifecycle policy) brought W4's p95 to 2,057 ms, 40 ms over W4's SLO, at the cost of the replies; W5 did not re-measure it. Without the change, the alternative is a GPU per workload.

These rows ran the box's step cap, 512 tokens a step with a 256-token threshold. The serving profile runs the change at 256 tokens a step without a threshold, which five later rounds on new samples confirmed: 44 of 295 replies over each round's SLO against 94 at 512/256, at 7% less of the agents' token rate ([the step cap](BENCHMARKS.md#the-step-cap-screened-and-confirmed-sw-rg)).

On an A100 SXM4 80 GB held to the box's pool, model and agents, the same configuration took the voice p95 from 4,986 to 1,634 ms, under that card's SLO of 1,669 ms in each of five rounds, and the agents' throughput held ([the A100 run](HARDWARE.md#what-it-measured-2026-10-26)).

Numbers this page labels W4 ran the v0.3 variant, with three gaps that W5 fixed ([ARCHITECTURE.md §2](ARCHITECTURE.md#2-the-change-63350-and-63351)), and include their effect. W5's, which this page gives otherwise, ran the fixed variant; beside v0.3's in W5's campaign it differed by +8 ms in V2V p95 at the median of the five rounds' differences ([BENCHMARKS.md](BENCHMARKS.md#the-discriminating-scenario-after-w5s-fixes-w5)).

## Is your deployment in this regime?

The change matters when background KV fills the pool: agent contexts of 10k tokens or more against a pool that holds a few of them. On stock vLLM the signs are KV cache usage near 1 (`vllm:kv_cache_usage_perc`) while requests wait (`vllm:num_requests_waiting`), and a realtime time to first token of seconds at p95. Where the pool has room, the change does less: at the standard scenario's realistic load (16 agent sessions of the whole corpus) the KV preemption has little to do (0–7 preemptions a run), and the budget cap still keeps agent prefill out of each reply's first tokens. Over five rounds, each on its own sample of the agents, the PRs' configuration had the lower p95 in every round, 2.02–2.42 s against tuned vLLM's 2.15–3.83 s, and met the SLO in one ([BENCHMARKS.md](BENCHMARKS.md#the-standard-scenario-in-five-rounds-w5)).

### Can the served model do the background work?

Sharing the card shares the model: the background work runs on the model the call needs. Check that it does that work
well enough before sharing a card for it. On a useful pair of jobs, answering questions about private documents in a
call (QASPER) beside enriching new documents into searchable records (Re-DocRED), the served Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507-FP8
failed nine of the quality gate's eleven criteria, both tasks' central ones among them: 50.6% of the questions it
answered were answered correctly, against 80%, and 23.9% of the facts in its records were right, against 80%. The
screen failed as well with an FP8 KV cache and with Qwen3-8B-FP8 on a card of its own
([the quality record](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-27-quality/README.md); [`benchmarks/quality`](../benchmarks/quality)
runs it on another model or pair). The measurements on this page replay coding agents' traffic, which needs no
answers to be right; they say what sharing costs the call, not whether the shared model's background answers are worth
keeping.

## The configuration

### The engine

- **Priority scheduling with a step cap**: `--scheduling-policy priority --max-num-batched-tokens 256 --long-prefill-token-threshold 0`, the serving profile's: steps of at most 256 tokens and no per-request prefill cap, the step cap the sweep chose for the change ([its screens](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-27-sweep/README.md)). The step cap bounds how long a running decode waits behind a prefill chunk. The frozen box keeps its own, 512/256 in [`engine.yaml`](../deploy/phastform/engine.yaml), as the baseline every measurement changes, and the serving variant passes the profile's over it. A faster card's own cap may be larger, and with the change the cap sets one wait it cannot remove: a voice turn that arrives with steps in flight waits for them. On an A100 SXM4 80 GB, vLLM's default 2,048/0 gave the agents 1.4 times the tokens a second of 512/256, and with the PRs the voice p95 stayed 0.14–0.17 s over its SLO at all of that throughput, against under it at 512/256 ([the A100 run](HARDWARE.md#what-it-measured-2026-10-26)): pick the cap by the voice SLO's margin.
- **Prefix caching and a CPU KV tier written through**: vLLM's `OffloadingConnector` with `offload_prompt_only: false`, about four times the GPU pool ([`engine.yaml`](../deploy/phastform/engine.yaml) has the frozen box's). A preempted agent can then reload its KV from host memory rather than recompute it: 509 ms against 3.26 s for a 16k-token context ([E0](BENCHMARKS.md#tier-cost-model-resuming-an-agent-turn-e0)); how much of a resume under E2's load was restored and how much recomputed, no vLLM 0.32 counter shows ([E3](BENCHMARKS.md#e3-what-make-room-takes-from-the-agents)).
- **A fixed KV pool** (`--kv-cache-memory`) when the audio models share the GPU, so the pool depends neither on the profiler nor on the audio servers' memory at start.
- **The change**, proposed to vLLM as pull requests [#63350](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/63350) and [#63351](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/63351), open and under review:
  - a waiting request of a higher priority preempts running requests of a lower priority when it lacks KV, at most 2 a step: `--max-priority-preemptions-per-step 2`;
  - prefills of a lower priority sit out the steps of its first tokens: `--priority-prefill-budget-cap --priority-prefill-budget-cap-tokens 48` (below for the 48).

  On vLLM 0.32.0, the [`make-room` variant](../deploy/phastform/variants/README.md) generates the same rules from the installed vLLM, without modifying it (`python python/flatshare_vllm/src/flatshare_vllm/build.py make-room OUT_DIR`), and the engine loads it with `PYTHONPATH=OUT_DIR` and `--scheduler-cls flatshare_make_room_scheduler.AsyncScheduler`. Its switches are keys of `--additional-config`: `make_room.max_preemptions_per_step 2`, `make_room.budget_cap true`, `make_room.budget_cap_tokens 48` and `make_room.reserve_tokens 0`. The repository's serving variant, [`variants/make-room-serve`](../deploy/phastform/variants/make-room-serve/flatshare-engine.conf), runs exactly that, rendered from the serving profile [`deploy/profiles/rtx-5060-ti-serve.toml`](../deploy/profiles/rtx-5060-ti-serve.toml), whose [`serving.json`](../deploy/phastform/serving.json) holds every setting of this section with the revisions it needs: the vLLM version, the model's and the tokenizer's revisions, the SHA-256 of the scheduler module and of the patches it is built from. Each start of the engine checks the configuration the engine reports from inside against it and fails on any difference ([the serving profile](../deploy/phastform/variants/README.md#the-serving-profile)); `variants/make-room` keeps arm 9's configuration, the reserve and a 16-token window, for the experiments. The variants refuse any other vLLM version, since their patches match 0.32.0's source, and a bad `make_room` key or value fails the engine start, naming it.

  E2 measured the PRs' configuration as this variant with these switch values; ported to 0.32.0, the PRs' diff schedules identically to it ([GLOSSARY.md](GLOSSARY.md#the-change-and-its-parts)). Since #63351's revision of 2026-10-25, the PR and the variant both count a request's first tokens in flight under asynchronous scheduling, which the frozen box runs; W5's measurements ran the earlier count, which held a step past the window ([after W5](BENCHMARKS.md#after-w5-the-first-tokens-in-flight)).
- **No reserve.** The variant's third switch keeps KV free for priority 0; beside the KV preemption it showed no effect: with a 48-token window, 2,205 ms (2,098–2,289) with it and the lifecycle policy against 2,193 ms (2,168–2,286) for the PRs' switches alone, three runs each on W4's one sample of the agents' traces, which leave a difference of up to about 0.2 s either way unresolved. The PRs leave it out.

### The priorities

Voice requests go at priority 0 and background work at 2; in vLLM a lower value is served first. A client can send `priority` in the request body or the `X-Vllm-Priority` header. A gateway in front should set it from the caller's identity instead, so that a background client cannot raise its own; `flatshare-gateway` maps each tenant to a class and caps it with `max_class`. Nothing else in front is needed: the measured configuration ran without an admission policy.

`flatshare-gateway` is the harness that measured it, not a production gateway: what one would add is listed in [its README](../crates/flatshare-gateway/README.md#what-it-does-not-do), and the engine must be reachable only through the gateway. vLLM's `--api-key` guards only its `/v1`, `/v2`, `/inference` and `/cohere` routes, so serve the engine on a Unix socket in a directory only its user may enter (`--uds`, as [`deploy/phastform/systemd`](../deploy/phastform/systemd) does) and reach it from the gateway on that socket, or through a tunnel that may reach nothing else ([ADR-0005](adr/0005-loopback-services-and-node-sticky-kv.md#unix-sockets-end-to-end-w6-2026-10-25)).

### The reply's first tokens

The budget cap protects a reply for its first N tokens. The caller hears nothing until the reply's first complete sentence reaches TTS (the voice agent hands TTS whole sentences, a sentence once it reaches 20 characters, LiveKit's default), so N must cover the first sentence, not the first token. A reply whose first sentence is longer decodes the rest of it beside agent prefill again: its tokens come 60–65 ms apart instead of 16–20 ms ([the prefill case](BENCHMARKS.md#the-prs-configuration-in-one-session-w5)). Choose N to cover your agent's first sentence at its longest; this voice agent is prompted for "one or two short, natural sentences" ([`worker.py`](../python/flatshare_voice/src/flatshare_voice/worker.py)), and an agent prompted for longer answers needs a longer window. This voice agent's replies run 23 tokens at the median and 52 at most (W5's 5,571 replies):
- at N = 16 with full replies the p95 is 3,094 ms (2,868–3,192);
- at N = 48 it is 2,236 ms (2,132–2,397, fifteen runs);
- at N = 16 with every reply capped at 16 tokens (`max_tokens`) it was 2,057 ms in W4, two of five runs under W4's SLO, at the price of truncated replies. That cap was arm 9's degrade, which capped every reply; the gateway's `realtime_max_tokens` sets such a cap on every realtime reply by itself, with or without an admission policy.

A longer window holds background prefill for longer each turn: the agents' throughput is 2% lower at 48 than at 16 (3,236 against 3,298 tokens/s over the calls' first 240 s, in the same rounds of W5's PR campaign).

A sweep of the window against whole replies of 40 tokens, beside the box's closed-loop agents, places the knee ([`knee.sh`'s records](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-25-window-knee/README.md), 2026-10-25): the reply's 40th token came 3,759 ms after the request at p95 with a 16-token window, 2,642 at 24, 1,854 at 32 and 1,037 at 48, and no sooner at 64 (1,018 ms), while the first token stayed at 326–448 ms. Those agents' prompt throughput, all of it prefill, fell 8% from 16 to 48 and no further at 64: a hold ends when its reply ends, so a window longer than the replies costs no more than one just long enough. When in doubt, choose N on the long side of your agent's first sentence.

### Calls: what the box admits

The voice worker runs in one of two modes ([`worker.py`](../python/flatshare_voice/src/flatshare_voice/worker.py)). Bench mode, what every measurement ran, takes every call LiveKit dispatches. Serve mode, the serving configuration ([`deploy/cyan/variants/serve`](../deploy/cyan/variants/serve/flatshare-voice-worker.conf)), admits at most the serving profile's calls at once, two: the calls at which the sweep and the two-card rounds measured the profile, its envelope of agents beside them ([Two cards](#two-cards)); at W5's step cap, 4 and 6 callers were further over each one's SLO than two ([calls per GPU](BENCHMARKS.md#calls-per-gpu-w5)). Its load, which LiveKit's agents SDK reads every half second, is its calls over that number, with LiveKit's load threshold at 1, so a full worker is marked full and the server sends it no job; a job request beyond it is refused, counted exactly when requests arrive together. The SDK sends the server the worker's status only every 2.5 s, though (LiveKit Agents 1.8.8's `UPDATE_STATUS_INTERVAL`), and the server dispatches a room once: for up to 3 s after a call's process ends, a full worker is still held full, and a call that comes then gets no agent although a line is free: 2 of the 24-hour soak's 476 calls did ([the record](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-28-soak/README.md)). It takes no new call while the gateway's `/ready` says the engine does not serve (warming, degraded or drained, or 503 while the engine's `/health` fails; a gateway without the route leaves capacity alone to admit), nor while a release drains it. A refused call gets no agent: a client should take an agent that has not joined within a few seconds for a busy line. Calls in progress are never cut by admission.

### Real callers' audio

The benchmark's callers are synthetic: files played into the room, with no echo and no room noise. Serve mode's audio is the profile's other configuration, `microphone`, for real callers, which no benchmark measured: LiveKit's end-of-turn model ends a turn, since people pause inside a sentence (the experiments' fixed endpointing is refused with it); the VAD needs a clearer and longer onset to count speech (an activation of 0.6 against 0.5, 0.1 s against 0.05) and an interruption 0.6 s of speech, so that a click or a door does not cut the agent off, and an interruption that no transcript follows within 2 s lets the agent resume; and LiveKit's 3 s AEC warm-up stays on, so that a loudspeaker's echo of the agent does not cut its first reply. Noise suppression is the caller's client's (a browser's WebRTC applies its own), since LiveKit's noise cancellation needs LiveKit Cloud. Measure it with your own callers before relying on it. With fixed endpointing, as the measurements run it, a turn waits out the VAD's silence (0.55 s) and about 0.2 s more, and LiveKit's 0.5 s endpointing delay does not bind: 0.3 s of silence answered 0.27 s sooner at the median, and took the pause after a first sentence for the end of one line in 40 ([the endpointing sweep](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-28-endpointing/README.md)).

### The audio models

- Whisper and Kokoro share the GPU by time-slicing; MPS does not help ([Voice on the shared GPU](BENCHMARKS.md#voice-on-the-shared-gpu)): the interference that matters is inside the LLM engine. Sharing still costs them a little: in the shared configurations of [What it buys](#what-it-buys), endpointing takes 822–825 ms instead of 756, and TTS's first audio 232–250 ms instead of 211 (means over W5's replies, from [W5's report](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-24-e2-w5/report/frontier.md#stage-waterfall)).
- Pin each server to its own CPU cores: the engine on the GPU's NUMA node, the audio servers off it, as the box's units do ([`deploy/phastform/systemd`](../deploy/phastform/systemd)).
- Keep them on the GPU. On the CPU they would give their 3.4 GiB to the KV pool, but Kokoro's first audio took 991 ms at the median with one call and 2,736 ms with two, against 211–242 ms for the GPU's whole TTS stage, and a Whisper transcript 9 s or more, on a 1.8 GHz Xeon faster than the box's ([the CPU record](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-27-cpu-audio/README.md)).

## Two cards

With two cards, put both calls on one and fill the other with agents. A resource gate fixed before the runs compared the arrangements of two RTX 5060 Ti cards in three rounds, each on a new sample of the agents and on the two hosts in turn ([RESOURCE-GATE.md](../benchmarks/quality/RESOURCE-GATE.md); [the rounds' record](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-28-rounds/README.md); [the report, §6.8](REPORT.md#68-on-two-cards-consolidating-both-calls-on-one-does-34-more-background-work-most-of-it-without-the-change)). An arrangement qualifies when at most 5% of its replies are over the SLO, voice alone's p95 plus 250 ms on that card:
- **A card per workload** (the calls on one, agents alone on the other) qualifies, and its agents' card does the most at 14 sessions, 7,893 tokens a second over the calls' first 240 s: at 24 its pool thrashes and it does 4,232.
- **Consolidation**, both calls and at most four sessions on one card with the serving profile, beside the agents' card, did 10,596 tokens a second, 34% more, with 5 of 177 replies over the SLO (2.8%). At eight sessions 31 were over. On stock vLLM the shared card qualifies at two sessions only, and that arrangement did 27% more than a card per workload: the second card's agents dominate both, and the change's own share is the two sessions more it carries.
- **Pooling**, one call and 12 sessions a card, qualifies on neither engine: 24 of 87 replies over with the change, 53 of 87 on stock vLLM.
- **A warm spare.** In those arrangements the agents' card runs no speech servers and cannot take the calls. With them resident and the agents on the serving profile's engine, the second card did the most at 12 sessions, and consolidation at four with that spare did 9,652 tokens a second, 22% more than a card per workload, which has no standby.

The arrangement to deploy is consolidation with a warm spare. In `gateway.toml` ([`deploy/cyan/gateway.example.toml`](../deploy/cyan/gateway.example.toml) has it): a `[[nodes]]` entry per card, both with the realtime and background roles and `max_calls = 2`, the first with `max_agents = 4` and the spare with `max_agents = 12`, and `[placement] calls = "fill"`, which places each call on the first node with a free call slot in the file's order. A node with both roles must say how many agents' sessions it takes beside its calls, a count or `"unbounded"`: the gateway refuses to start without it. The runbook's [Two GPU nodes](../ops/RUNBOOK.md#two-gpu-nodes) says what each key does, and how the nodes' health, placement and failover work.

### The service contract

What the two cards promise at that configuration, and what they do not:
- **Calls accepted**: two at once in all, which the voice worker's serve mode admits ([Calls](#calls-what-the-box-admits)); each card's `max_calls` holds both, so that the spare can take them. A third call gets no agent: a client should take an agent that has not joined within a few seconds for a busy line. A call's turns are the voice agent's: one at a time, its replies one or two short sentences (23 tokens at the median and 52 at most in W5), the first sentence within the 48-token window, and interruptions answered.
- **Agents accepted**: 16 sessions at once, four on the first card and 12 on the spare. One more gets 503 `no_agent_slot` with `Retry-After: 1` until a session ends or idles out (`idle_s`, 150 s here, without a request), and every request is bounded per class and tenant by the gateway's intake, refused at once beyond it with `Retry-After`. A client that starts new sessions without ending the old ones holds the slots of those it has left until they idle out: end a session with a `session_closed` event, or offer a few sessions under the bounds ([the two-node smokes](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-28-two-node-smoke/README.md)). The bounds count agents' sessions: a background request that names none (no trusted `X-Session-ID`, `X-Claude-Code-Session-ID` or `prompt_cache_key`) takes no agent slot, so it runs only on a card that carries no call and holds fewer sessions than its `max_agents`, and gets 503 `no_agent_slot` when no card does ([Nodes](../crates/flatshare-gateway/README.md#nodes)).
- **The calls' outcome**: at most 5% of the replies over the SLO. The rounds measured 2.8% at four sessions; the 24-hour soak 1.5%, 74 of 5,058 replies, and 5.5% in its worst hour ([the record](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-28-soak/README.md)).
- **The agents' outcome**: their tokens a second and their time to first token, as measured: 9,652 tokens a second at the median of the rounds over the calls' first 240 s, a TTFT of 0.5 s at the median and 10.7 s at p99; over the 24-hour soak the agents ran at 3,529 tokens a second: 9,300 in its first hour, where the replay resumes every session at once and the rounds' windows sit, and 582–5,402 in each hour after, at their traces' own pace. These count the replay's tokens, not tasks done, and the served model did not pass the quality gate for useful background work ([above](#can-the-served-model-do-the-background-work)).
- **Overload**: refused, never queued without bound: a call beyond two, an agent's session beyond 16, a request beyond the intake's bounds. Nothing is deferred but the agents of a card that covers: the spare's while it carries any call, and a card's that carries calls moved to it from a failed one.
- **Recovery**: when the first card's engine or speech servers fail, its calls move to the spare by their next turn, and while the spare carries any call its agents' new requests wait for their class's queue timeout (120 s) and are then answered 503 `queue_timeout` (those already running finish), and the first card's agents' sessions find the spare at its bound and get 503 `no_agent_slot` until a slot frees. In the drills ([the record](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-28-node-failover/README.md)) both calls' next turns reached the spare 0.7 s after a SIGKILL to the first card's engine and 1.9 s after its stop, and none of the 300 turns after failed; the killed engine was back 101 s after the kill. Once the first card is healthy again it takes the new calls, the calls moved to the spare stay there until they end, and the spare's agents run again after them; there is no graceful drain of one card.
- **Not covered**: more than two calls at once, replies longer than the window, real callers' microphones (serve mode's audio, which no run measured), and an outage of both cards or of the control plane on cyan.

## What not to deploy

Measured in the same scenario:
- **Lifecycle-timed protection in a gateway** (KV headroom held from speech onset, a prefill throttle while a voice turn is answered, the voice agent's events): with agents' KV filling the pool these could not act, and without the change a gateway doing all of it left the p95 at 10,993 ms in W4, as stock. A gateway can make room only by aborting admitted work, at the agents' tail ([why](METHODS.md#what-lifecycle-timing-could-and-could-not-do)).
- **vLLM's development mode** (`VLLM_SERVER_DEV_MODE=1`), which only a measured box runs, from the measurement drop-in (`variant.sh measure on`), for the harness's `/server_info` and cache resets: it serves routes such as `/collective_rpc`, `/sleep` and `/update_weights`, which `--api-key` does not guard ([the routes](../deploy/phastform/variants/README.md#the-measurement-drop-in)).
- **KV cache events on TCP**: they carry every cached block's token ids and session id, and vLLM sends them to whoever binds the endpoint. The frozen box publishes none.
- **Engine knobs**: `--watermark` (no change), `--max-num-queued-tokens` (it refuses voice turns too: 48 of 50 failed), concurrency caps (two agent contexts fill the pool), [#54530](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/54530) (it preempts at the request-count cap, which does not bind here).
- **Another engine**: SGLang 0.5.22's priority preemption, with or without its host tier (11,948 ms with HiCache, five runs in W4).
- **A static KV reservation** halves the tail (5,671 ms in W4) but stays seconds over the SLO.
- **#63350 alone**: 5,085 ms (W5). The KV preemption without the budget cap gets the turn into the batch, and its reply then decodes beside agent prefill.
- **An FP8 KV cache beside the CPU tier** (`--kv-cache-dtype fp8`): it holds twice the tokens in the same memory (72,816 against 36,400) and cost no quality the gate's screen saw, but under E2's load the engine died both times it ran, on a CUDA fault at a step's output copy with a CPU-tier transfer in flight, on vLLM 0.32.0 on this card ([the sweep's record](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-27-sweep/README.md#the-fp8-kv-cache)).

Without any engine change, aborting background requests that have no first byte yet and resubmitting them (`flatshare-gateway`'s `preempt-resubmit` policy) holds 2,522 ms (2,516–2,868) at tuned vLLM's throughput (3,332 against 3,305 agents' tokens/s over the calls' first 240 s), at the cost of the agents' tail (TTFT p99 193 s against 106 s): of the configurations measured on stock vLLM, the nearest to the SLO. It cannot reach a request that is already decoding, which the change can.

## Releasing a change

`deploy/deploy.sh HOST` releases a commit to a host as an immutable release, `~/flatshare/releases/ID`, with a manifest of what it holds (every file's SHA-256, the lock files, the Python environments by key, the gateway's binary and where it was built, the models' and tokenizer's revisions, the scheduler's patches and module, the serving profile); the environments are built once per lock and linked by every release that has the same. It verifies the release before it switches (the files, each environment's imports, the binaries' versions, the scheduler module that the patches build on the installed vLLM, the drop-ins and models the box uses), drains what the switch would cut (the voice worker's calls in serve mode, the engine's requests), switches one link, restarts or reloads only the units whose inputs changed, checks them (the engine's configuration against the serving profile among them), and switches back by itself on any failure. `deploy.sh HOST --rollback` returns to the previous release by the same steps ([the runbook](../ops/RUNBOOK.md#deploy)).

## What to watch

- **Voice**: V2V p95 per call from the voice stack, and the realtime time to first token at p95 (the gateway's request log, or vLLM's per-request metrics). The voice agent's per-turn stage times split a slow turn into endpointing, the LLM's first sentence and TTS ([the stages](BENCHMARKS.md#voice-under-agent-load-e2)).
- **The engine**: KV usage, running and waiting requests, and preemptions; `vllm:num_preemptions_total` counts a victim when its next output reaches the frontend, so it lags. The CPU tier's loads beyond the requests' first scheduling show what preempted requests restored; what they recompute, no vLLM 0.32 counter shows, since it counts a request's computed prompt tokens at its first scheduling only ([E3](BENCHMARKS.md#e3-what-make-room-takes-from-the-agents)).
- **Background**: completed tokens a second and the agents' TTFT p99, where a preemption policy moves the cost.
- [`ops/`](../ops) has these as code: the Prometheus scrape configuration (the gateway, and the engine through it), the SLO's burn-rate alerts on the realtime first byte (95% within 250 ms) and the gateway's overhead, health alerts, and a Grafana dashboard ([the runbook](../ops/RUNBOOK.md#monitoring)).

## How it fails

- **A voice turn that two preemptions cannot make room for** waits as on stock: the change preempts at most `N` requests a step, and none if `N` would not do.
- **Only realtime work** leaves nothing to preempt: the engine schedules it by priority and arrival, as stock.
- **Many calls at once.** The budget cap holds lower-priority prefill while any realtime request's first tokens are due, so replies that overlap hold it continuously. The engine logs, at most every 10 s, the steps it held, the prefill tokens and admissions it held back, and its preemptions to make room: watch them.
  - **The limit.** On vLLM's real scheduler, on CPU, beside six agents' prefill, replies at least the 48-token window long left the agents 82% of stock's prompt tokens at one every 200 steps, 9% at one every 50 and none at one every 40, where each reply's window (about 49 steps) overlaps the next ([the test](../python/flatshare_vllm/tests/test_make_room.py)). At the 16–20 ms a step the box measures under the cap, that is about one realtime reply a second in aggregate, some six calls turning every 6 s. A 16-token window kept 91% at one every 40 steps.
  - **Up to six calls, measured.** E2 measured up to six calls with this voice agent, whose replies are nearly all shorter than the window. With 4 and 6 callers the PRs' p95 stayed 269 and 257 ms over each one's SLO at the median, against 202 ms with two; the cap held agent prefill back in 53% and 64% of the engine's steps, against a third, and the agents' throughput over the first 240 s fell to 3,055 and 2,716 tokens/s, from 3,203 ([calls per GPU](BENCHMARKS.md#calls-per-gpu-w5)).
  - **A datacenter batch.** On an A100, with 256 requests in flight and 26 of them at priority 0 in closed loop, the hold took the priority 2 requests' TTFT p99 from 1.1 s to 8.0 s while their median fell ([the A100 run](BENCHMARKS.md#the-a100-run-2026-10-26)).
- **A KV load that never completes** holds lower-priority prefill for its first `budget_cap_tokens` steps, then nothing.
- **The engine dies** (an out-of-memory kill, a crash): every stream it serves breaks at once. The gateway sends each event stream vLLM's error event, of type `upstream_unavailable`, before it aborts it, so a client that reads events learns why; the next request gets one 502, and the ones after it 503 `upstream_not_ready` with `Retry-After: 1` without reaching for the engine, until its `/health` answers. Voice is down for all of it: one GPU node has no standby. In serve mode the voice worker takes no new call meanwhile, as the gateway's `/ready` says.
  - **The restart.** systemd starts the unit RestartSec after its last process exits, so the delay is pure wait, and the units' `StartLimitBurst=3` in 30 minutes ends a crash loop whatever it is: the box's GPU servers wait 5 s (W8's drill ran with 15).
  - **The final build's drill** (SIGKILL to the engine's unit): each stream got the error event 8–11 ms after the kill; the main process was gone 0.13 s after it and the unit's last process 7.6 s after it (the GPU's teardown). The warm start listened 92.1 s after the restart, and the first request succeeded 106.2 s after the kill (112.3 s in W8's); on cold caches (a start after an upgrade) expect about nine minutes.
- **The gateway restarts.** A deploy takes a new gateway with `systemctl --user reload flatshare-gateway`: the gateway hands its listening sockets to a successor started from the new binary and configuration, which serves at once, and drains. No request is refused or waits; realtime and interactive requests in flight finish in the old process, and background ones are cut after `background_drain_s` (5 s) with a `draining` error event or a 503 with `Retry-After`, which their clients may send again. While a reload's predecessor drains, two admission points serve one engine, neither seeing the other's requests; the measured configuration has none.
  - **A restart or a crash.** `flatshare-gateway.socket` holds the sockets across both: a plain restart refuses nothing but answers the connections made during its drain only after it, and after a crash systemd starts the gateway again in about a second, the connections waiting meanwhile.
  - **The final build's drills.** A reload failed none of 42 requests, the slowest answered in 0.13 s, with its voice turns whole; a restart failed none either, the requests sent during its drain answered once it ended, 20.7 s on; and a kill none, the slowest answered in 1.3 s.
- **Host memory pressure.** The CPU tier lives in shared memory charged to the engine's cgroup, so a memory-pressure killer such as `systemd-oomd` picks the engine first under a host-wide stall ([the runbook](../ops/RUNBOOK.md#failures)). Keep the host's swap and memory pressure low; where `systemd-oomd` acts for the engine's own manager, `ManagedOOMPreference=avoid` on the engine's unit makes it a last resort.
- **Another process on the GPU** slows every stage: keep the GPU exclusive, as `gpu-guard` does for the box's units.
- **A reserve too large to start** (only with `make_room.reserve_tokens` above 0): the engine refuses to start if the reserve and one `max_model_len` request do not fit in the pool together.

## Checking it on your hardware

- `python/flatshare_vllm/src/flatshare_vllm/make-room/probe.py ENGINE kv` sends a voice-sized request at priority 0 while long priority-2 requests hold the pool, and `probe.py ENGINE prefill` sends it behind three long prefills; each sizes its requests from the engine's pool and context, takes about a minute and prints the turn's first token and its 16th, with the engine's state before and after (`--reply 40`: a whole 40-token reply, timed to its last token too). ENGINE is the engine's address, `unix:` and its socket (the GPU node's, by default) or `http://host:port`.
- `tail.py ENGINE SECONDS [AGENTS] [fresh|grow] [--reply TOKENS]` runs closed-loop agents beside two calls, by default as many as keep the frozen box's KV pressure on your pool (12 on the box's), and prints the calls' TTFT distribution and the agents' throughput; `--reply` times whole replies to their 16th and last token.
- The whole measurement: an E2 arm file with your traces and `flatshare-bench e2 run` ([benchmarks/e2](../benchmarks/e2/README.md)).

The change itself has two checks a checkout runs on CPU ([python/flatshare_vllm](../python/flatshare_vllm/README.md#upkeep-the-changes-cost-and-its-fit-upstream) says how to read them). `make perf-make-room TORCH_PYTHON=<CPU torch 2.13 with vLLM 0.32.0's dependencies>` times what the change costs a scheduler step with its switches off, against stock vLLM's step, and in the configuration to deploy, against the step with them off, in the 13 states of the PRs' cost tables, against the baseline measured on peacock, a 2.1 GHz Xeon. Another CPU weights the change's code differently (on an M5 Max, two states came 3.5% and 3.6% above the Xeon's, half its 7% tolerance), so measure your machine's own baseline first (`make perf-make-room UPDATE=1 PERF_BASELINE=build/perf-baseline.json PERF_BESIDE=<what else ran>`), then check against it (`PERF_BASELINE=build/perf-baseline.json`) after changing the engine's code.

Before moving off vLLM 0.32.0, `make compat-upstream` says whether #63350 and #63351 still merge into vLLM's main (with `TORCH_PYTHON`, an interpreter for main, whether their tests pass there) and whether make-room's patches apply to the newest release: they apply to 0.32.0 alone, by design, and where they do not, it names the first hunk that fails, where the backport needs rebasing.
